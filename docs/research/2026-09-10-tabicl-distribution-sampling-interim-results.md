# TabICL in CounterContEx — interim results and conclusions

Date: 2026-09-10

## Evidence scope and status

The evidence collected so far comes from two sources:

1. the controlled E3 test-partition experiment, which compared TabICL with a simple empirical
   backend using the same nine-quantile grid and search procedure;
2. the new E11-E13 pilots on 20 deterministically selected validation factuals from each of the
   four retained datasets.

The empirical backend constructs global marginal proposals. For a numerical feature, it returns
quantiles calculated from reference observations that the classifier assigns to the target
class. For a categorical feature, it uses category frequencies. Two factuals with the same
target therefore receive the same proposals. TabICL produces conditional proposals that depend
on the current factual, its other feature values, the target class, and the model context.

The E11-E13 pilots provide mechanism evidence and an estimate of confirmation cost. They are
not yet held-out test-partition confirmation. Stage 7 also remains formally marked `BLOCKED`:
the authorization record required by the frozen protocol was created after the first GPU run.
The artifacts and their hashes were verified successfully, but this procedural deviation cannot
be corrected retrospectively.

## 1. E3: TabICL versus the empirical backend

E3 found no broad advantage for TabICL. Relative to the empirical backend, TabICL had:

- slightly higher threshold validity: `+0.0057`;
- slightly better neighbour support: distance lower by `0.0004`;
- slightly lower coverage: `-0.0033`;
- worse Gower proximity: distance higher by `0.0079`;
- `8.09×` longer runtime.

The threshold-validity gain was driven mainly by Lending Club, while Gower distance worsened on
five of the six datasets. These results do not support a general claim that TabICL's conditional
proposals improve final counterfactuals.

At the same time, it would be too broad to claim that “TabICL provides no advantage.” E3
evaluated TabICL through a nine-quantile interface, not the full quality of the distribution
generated by the model. The result therefore applies to a specific combination: TabICL, nine
quantiles, projection into the legal feature space, and the current search procedure.

## 2. E3 diagnostic: is TabICL genuinely conditional?

The diagnostic confirmed that TabICL does not accidentally collapse to the global empirical
distribution:

- `2519/2520` raw quantile values differed between the two backends;
- after another feature was changed, every examined TabICL quantile responded, while empirical
  proposals remained unchanged;
- in the fresh HELOC replay, `1530/1530` raw values differed, and subsequent projection erased
  `313` of those differences.

However, distributional differences often did not propagate to the final result. Projection
removed some of them, while search frequently found solutions through the same categorical
edits before the different numerical proposals could affect the valid candidate pool.

The most accurate conclusion from E3 is:

> TabICL supplies factual-dependent proposals, while the empirical backend supplies global
> marginal proposals. Richer conditioning helps only when it places more candidates inside a
> low-cost valid region that the search can exploit.

dataset-dependent and does not by itself solve the problem of finding a valid region.

## 4. E12: feature distributions and classifier response

E12 passed single-feature proposals through LR, MLP, and XGBoost classifiers and measured the
absolute change in target-class probability. The contrast
`D = categorical effect - numerical effect` was calculated only for factuals for which both
feature types were estimable.

E12 does not run counterfactual search or return a final counterfactual set. Final-CF coverage,
validity, Gower, numerical L2, categorical proximity, sparsity, LOF, Isolation Forest, and
diversity are therefore `N/A`, not zero. The applicable E12 quality measure is the change in
target probability. The final resume took `495 s` for 27 new cells plus re-verification of all
36 cells. Comparable phase-level and per-cell runtime are `NOT MEASURED`.

The equal-dataset mean values of `D` were:

| Classifier | Mean D |
|---|---:|
| LR | `+0.0805` |
| MLP | `+0.0986` |
| XGBoost | `+0.0441` |

For every classifier, the contrast was positive on two of the three datasets containing both
feature types and negative on Bank Marketing. The clearest positive bootstrap evidence occurred
for LR with target 0 (`+0.1170`, 95% interval `[+0.0954,+0.1509]`) and XGBoost with target 0
(`+0.0656`, `[+0.0419,+0.0947]`). The remaining intervals were heterogeneous or included zero.

The hypothesis that categorical changes have a larger effect therefore received partial support,
but it is not a general rule. The result depends on the dataset, classifier, and target class.
Many Give Me Some Credit factuals did not have an estimable contrast because of high no-op mass
and limited conditional support for alternative categories. HELOC has no categorical features
under this protocol and therefore provides no evidence for the contrast.

## 5. E13: sampling from the pi-beta distribution

E13 evaluated sampling from

\[
\pi_\beta(z) \propto q_{\mathrm{TabICL}}(z)\,
p_{\mathrm{clf}}(y_t \mid x_{-j}, z)^\beta,
\]

without an external cost proxy. At each expansion, a shared pool of `M=64` candidates was first
sampled from `q_TabICL`. Unique rows were scored by the classifier, after which `B=9` candidates
were sampled using target-class probabilities as weights.

Pairwise diversity is also `N/A` because E13 uses `k=1`. Aggregate E13 phase times were
`0.45 s` for preparation, `1086.28 s` for generation, `3.90 s` for evaluation, `6.64 s` for
writing, and `1105.33 s` in total.

For the primary comparison of `beta=1` against `beta=0`:

- threshold success changed by `-0.0042`;
- coverage did not change;
- Gower improved by `0.0104`;
- the number of changed action units fell by `0.131`;
- numerical L2 improved by `0.0023`;
- categorical proximity improved by `0.1208` groups;
- LOF improved by `0.2167`, while Isolation Forest worsened by `0.0013`;
- NN-5 worsened by approximately `0.0005`.

The mechanism behaved as defined. In the controlled comparison at identical states, all
`3300/3300` action keys had the same q pools, classifier scores, and random numbers across beta
values. Mean selected-candidate probability increased monotonically from `.26027` at beta 0 to
`.26416` at beta 1 and `.26720` at beta 2. Effective sample size nevertheless remained high
(`63.18` for beta 1 with M=64), showing that the reweighting was usually weak. Better local
selections did not translate into higher final validity in adaptive search.

The operational `classifier-top-B` variant, which deterministically selects the candidates with
the highest target probabilities from the pool, achieved threshold success `+0.1583` higher
than beta 0. This is an important mechanism signal, but classifier-top-B is not a pi-beta sample.
It remains a secondary comparator rather than a confirmation arm selected after observing the
pilot.

## Conclusions so far

1. **TabICL genuinely models conditional proposals.** Its feature distributions differ greatly
   from the empirical backend and respond to the other values in a factual.
2. **Conditionality does not guarantee better counterfactuals.** Projection, no-ops, duplicates,
   limited categorical support, and search policy can erase or bypass information contained in q.
3. **There is not yet enough evidence for a broad claim either that TabICL has an advantage or
   that it has no advantage.** E3 restricted the model to nine quantiles, while E11-E13 are small
   validation pilots.
4. **IID sampling from q is promising, but the effect is heterogeneous.** In E11 it improved mean
   validity, coverage, Gower, categorical proximity, and sparsity relative to the grid. The
   numerical-L2 change was negligible, LOF and Isolation Forest did not agree, and neighbour
   support worsened. Quality gains came from individual datasets.
5. **Categorical changes can have a larger effect, but not always.** The average contrast was
   positive for all three classifier families, yet Bank Marketing consistently contradicted the
   hypothesis and some factuals were not estimable.
6. **Soft classifier weighting improves several local quality metrics, but beta=1 did not improve
   final validity.** Gower, numerical L2, categorical proximity, sparsity, and LOF improved, while
   Isolation Forest and NN-5 did not. High ESS suggests that q and classifier scores were not
   sufficiently discriminative at M=64 and the tested beta values.
7. **Explicit classifier selection is the strongest signal observed so far.** The classifier-top-B
   result suggests that the bottleneck may lie more in selection strength and interaction with
   search than in the absence of useful information in the TabICL distribution. A separately
   predeclared experiment is required before making a quality claim from this result.

## Next step

The next valid step is separately authorized confirmation of the frozen E11-E13 protocols on
100 test factuals and five seeds. The estimated cost of E11-E13 at `k=1` is 10-15 aggregate GPU
hours on the host used for the pilots. The `k=3` replication is a separate scope and is not
included in this estimate. Until confirmation, these results must be described as pilot evidence,
and no arm should be selected based on the observed outcomes.
